# Phase two: tiled rendering and real RGB-D pilot

This stage extends the independent core, not the full author system. It does not reproduce ORB-SLAM3/MODP tracking, online LM, Hessian pruning, Gaussian densification, AccuTile or fused CUDA/Metal kernels.

## Rendering

- `romo_gs/tiled.py` supports rectangular images and calibrated fx/fy/cx/cy. Projection adds0.3px² low-pass covariance and rejects Gaussian centers at depth≤0.05m.
- Sort Gaussian centers front-to-back once per frame. Build conservative axis-aligned support bounds: sqrt(24·diag(Σ′)) + |v′|T/2 +1pixel. The additional margin covers pixel/tile boundary rounding. This is conservative linear-sweep binning, not the paper's row-wise AccuTile method.
- Tiles32×32, Gaussian chunks256 for the primary experiments. Stream accumulated transmittance between depth-ordered chunks. No early termination. Culling support uses detached metadata; sorting and bin membership are discrete.
- `q_cutoff=None` disables support culling to compare exact dense and tiled values/gradients. With q_cutoff24, very small Gaussian tails outside intersecting tiles are dropped; the actual error is measured on real-data subsets rather than assumed to be zero.
- Midpoint sampling composites at each time before averaging. Process5 samples per tensor batch, preserving exactly the same10-point quadrature as the independent phase-one time loop. Analytic rendering integrates each Gaussian opacity before compositing and retains its known model approximation.
- Training uses non-reentrant activation checkpointing per tile. Forward saves selected projected parameters, not all Gaussian×pixel footprints. Backward recomputes a tile, trading compute for lower retained memory. Gaussian chunks alone would not bound full-image backward memory because autograd can retain all chunks.
- Geometry changes within the exposure, depth-order changes and covariance motion are not modeled by either screen-space training renderer. Controlled observations are instead generated by an independent perspective depth-reprojection process, so they are not labels from the model under test.

## Data and calibration

Two TUM RGB-D sequences, `freiburg1_xyz` and `freiburg1_desk`, from official TUM archives. Full archiveSHA-256, selected frame paths, individual source hashes, timestamps and RGB/depth synchronization offsets are recorded in `results/phase2/*_source.json`.

For each sequence, select the first RGB frame at least5seconds after the initial timestamp, then15 frames at stride3. Train indices0/1/3/4/6/7/9/10/12/13; test2/5/8/11/14. These are fixed temporal subsets, not selected for model quality. No test RGB or depth contributes to initialization or optimization.

Use the ROS default525/525/319.5/239.5 calibration without undistortion, as TUM recommends for its pre-registered RGB-D. Intrinsics follow half-pixel-correct image resizing to96×72. Depth is uint16/5000 metres; zero is invalid and the already-applied TUM depth scale correction is not applied a second time. RGB uses area/BOX resizing via320×240; depth uses nearest-neighbor. Valid depth0.2–5.0m. Mocap camera-to-world poses are interpolated in position and quaternionSLERP. Velocity is estimated around each selected frame at±0.05seconds and expressed in the frame's camera coordinates.

## Two observation tracks

1. **Native:** fit the original downsampled RGB at known mocap poses. Exposure1/60s is an explicitly assumed setting, because the dataset does not supply sensor exposure for these frames. Evaluate the predicted observation against held-out original RGB. A center-render comparison is retained as a diagnostic. This track does not establish real sensor deblurring quality.
2. **Controlled:** add0.08s blur to each original RGB-D frame, using its measured local velocity. Independently reproject the real320×240 RGB-D point cloud under continuous rigid camera motion, with bilinear splatting and a nearest-surface z-buffer. Average32 midpoint exposures, then area-downsample to96×72. The original frame is the pre-additional-blur reference; any native blur already present remains. This is controlled additional blur on a real scene, not a claim of perfectly sharp sensor ground truth.

Controlled masks require valid depth, sufficient bilinear support at every sample, valid area-downsampling support and one-pixel erosion. The fixed data mask is identical for both methods and all seeds; low-opacity or poorly reconstructed pixels are not removed by a prediction-dependent mask. Its coverage is limited, especially on desk, and is reported per frame and in the results. Identity reprojection and32-vs64 exposure integration are audited separately.

## Initialization and optimization

Fuse training depth points in2.5cm voxels; deterministic seed20261117 selects at most2400 voxel representatives. Initial sigma is max(0.8pixel projected world scale, min(0.8nearest-selected-point spacing,0.05m)). This spacing term was introduced after an explicitly retained exploratory run exposed inadequate initial coverage with pixel-only scale; it is an implementation choice, not an author-specified rule.

Fuse colors from the corresponding training observation track. The controlled track uses only its blurred training RGB; it never initializes colors from clean/pre-blur RGB. Points without valid color support start at neutral gray. Positions and depth geometry come from training depth only. Both methods use identical paired initial parameters and Gaussian count.

Optimize position offsets bounded to±3cm per coordinate, diagonal world-covariance log-scale changes bounded to±0.7, SH0 RGB logits and opacity logits. Initial opacity0.8; color-logit noiseN(0,0.03) with seeds0/1/2. Camera states fixed. Adam learning rates: position0.015, log scale0.015, RGB0.04, opacity0.02. Loss0.8maskedL1 +0.2masked valid7×7 uniform-windowSSIM +1e−4mean squared position-offset parameter. Cycle10 training views deterministically.

The three seeds perturb color initialization only; geometry, data, fusion selection and view order are shared. Their standard deviation measures this optimization perturbation, not independent scene variation or full pipeline uncertainty.

The formal MPS cohort uses both methods, both tracks, both sequences and three seeds. CPU uses the paired seed0 controlled cases for device feasibility. Hardware allocation is chosen from the isolated profile results before formal training, without selecting on reconstruction quality. Each run uses150steps, a fixed short pilot budget; there is no claim of full convergence. Final-state parameters and losses are retained instead of selecting the best held-out checkpoint. Historical5-step smoke runs and a60-step spacing trial remain separate from formal results.

## Metrics

- RGB PSNR: peak1, pooled RGB MSE across the five held-out views on fixed valid pixels. Controlled track evaluates center render against original pre-additional-blur RGB; native track evaluates exposure-rendered observation against original RGB. Also retain center and observation metrics separately.
- SSIM: mean over valid7×7 uniform windows and RGB channels, C1=.01²,C2=.03². Report per-view and per-seed values, then mean/sampleSD over3MPS seeds. No three-seed error bar is assigned to the one-seed CPU check.
- Depth RMSE: center-render expected depth (opacity-weighted depth / accumulated opacity) against held-out depth on the same data mask. Missing predictions are not excluded. This metric is geometric consistency at observed views, not surface Chamfer distance.
- Alpha90 coverage: fraction of fixed evaluation-mask pixels with accumulated opacity>0.9, reported independently rather than used to restrict PSNR.
- Full native-valid centerPSNR: original RGB reference on all valid-depth pixels, including regions excluded by the controlled-blur support mask. This broader diagnostic exposes reconstruction gaps outside the narrower controlled ROI; it is reported alongside ROI quality.

## Speed and memory

Primary performance measurements run before training, with a new process per case and no competing training processes. One real initialized view,2400Gaussians,96×72, CPU1thread/MPS, both methods, dense/tiled-retained/tiled-checkpointed backends. Different tile sizes16/32/48 are reported. One warmup plus three timed repetitions; synchronize MPS before/after forward/backward. Differentiate all position, full covariance, color and opacity inputs. Timings include projection and CPU bin planning. These are renderer measurements, not end-to-end SLAM FPS.

After training finishes, separately benchmark all12seed0final maps in fresh sequential processes. Inference uses15timings across5held-out views after one warmup per view; forward/backward uses3timings at the first training view. The renderer benchmark loss is mean square of all5output channels (RGB, weighted depth and alpha), not the complete photometric optimization step. Final-map measurements are distinguished from the matched-initial-geometry backend study because the two methods have learned slightly different parameters.

Memory has three distinct meanings:

- Process RSS high-water since isolated process startup, including the interpreter and data loading. On macOS this does not fully describe all GPU buffers.
- MPS live/driver allocation sampled every2ms, with baseline and peak increment. Polling can miss short peaks; these are observed peaks, not exact hardware counters. Driver allocation includes caching.
- Unique storage bytes retained by autograd after forward, measured with saved-tensor hooks in a separate pre-timing pass. This is not the complete process peak.

Apple unified-memory quantities overlap and must not be added. CPU and GPU memory figures are labeled separately. Training may use two CPU workers and two MPS workers concurrently to finish the cohort; its wall-clock timings are operational logs and are not the isolated speed comparison.

## Reproducibility and disclosure

Archive/source/prepared-data hashes, all configurations,150step logs, final parameter arrays, per-view metrics and replay checks are preserved. Original and processed RGB-D observations remain ignored local caches with download/rebuild instructions; they are not uploaded to the collection.
